Actium Resources
Yes—there are several useful public-domain secondary works available in full text, not just Plutarch, Dio, or the other ancient authors. The strongest starting points are Shuckburgh’s Augustus for Octavian and an explicitly listed index, and Weigall’s Cleopatra for sustained coverage of Antony, Cleopatra, and the Actium campaign. [en.wikisource](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Augustus_(Shuckburgh))

I’ve kept nonfiction first and distinguished a printed **index** from a table of contents or searchable text. Where I could not verify an index, I’ve said so.

## Best matches

### 1. Evelyn S. Shuckburgh — *Augustus: The Life and Times of the Founder of the Roman Empire (B.C. 63–A.D. 14)* (1903)

- Read online: [Wikisource text and contents](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Augustus_(Shuckburgh)).
- Alternative: [Internet Archive scan of the 1908 edition](https://archive.org/details/augustuslifetime00shuc), with PDF, EPUB, and full-text download options.
- Index: confirmed; the 1903 contents list it beginning on printed page 303.
- Coverage: Octavian’s inheritance from Caesar, the triumvirate, Philippi, Perusia and Sicily, and the transition to the new constitution after 30 BC, followed by Augustus’s government and later life. This is the best verified indexed biography in this shortlist for following Octavian across the entire period rather than isolating Actium. [en.wikisource](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Augustus_(Shuckburgh))

It is Shuckburgh’s own historical biography—not his edition of Suetonius—and therefore fits your request for material outside the classical texts. [en.wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evelyn_Shirley_Shuckburgh)

### 2. Arthur E. P. Brome Weigall — *The Life and Times of Cleopatra, Queen of Egypt: A Study in the Origin of the Roman Empire* (1914)

- Read online: [complete Project Gutenberg HTML text](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/54038/54038-h/54038-h.htm).
- Download formats: [Project Gutenberg catalog entry](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/54038).
- Alternative scan: [Internet Archive’s 1924 edition](https://archive.org/details/lifetimesofcleop00weig_0).
- Index: no separate subject index is listed in the Gutenberg edition’s contents; it has a detailed chapter list, searchable text, maps, illustrations, and a Ptolemaic genealogy.
- Coverage: the second half is devoted to Cleopatra and Antony, with successive chapters on preparations against Octavian, Antony’s decline, Actium, the return to Egypt, and their deaths. [gutenberg](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/54038/54038-h/54038-h.htm)

For your specific topic, these are the useful entry points; the page numbers refer to the 1914 printed edition:

| Chapter | Subject | Printed page |
|---|---|---:|
| XI | Antony’s character and rise to power | 203 |
| XV | Preparations for the overthrow of Octavian | 279 |
| XVI | Decline of Antony’s power | 303 |
| XVII | Battle of Actium and flight to Egypt | 324 |
| XIX | Octavian’s invasion of Egypt and Antony’s death | 368 |
| XX | Cleopatra’s death and Octavian’s triumph | 386 |

All are explicitly identified in the book’s contents. [gutenberg](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/54038/54038-h/54038-h.htm)

One important research limitation: Weigall openly says that he has minimized notes and references, and that his general interpretation is substantially new and open to discussion. He also deliberately challenges the hostile portrayal of Cleopatra inherited from her opponents. That makes it a useful contrasting interpretation, but a less convenient source for tracing every statement back to evidence. [gutenberg](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/54038/54038-h/54038-h.htm)

### 3. Evelyn S. Shuckburgh — *A History of Rome to the Battle of Actium* (1894; later editions)

- Read online: [Internet Archive’s 1894 digitization](https://archive.org/details/ahistoryrometob00shucgoog).
- Another edition: [Google Books record for the 1917 edition](https://books.google.com/books/about/A_History_of_Rome_to_the_Battle_of_Actiu.html?id=sqopAAAAYAAJ).
- Index: not verified in the retrieved material.
- Coverage: a general history of Rome ending at Actium, rather than a book devoted exclusively to the battle; the 1917 edition runs to 809 pages. It is the broader companion to Shuckburgh’s Augustus biography when you want the Republican background. [archive](https://archive.org/details/ahistoryrometob00shucgoog)

## A substantial secondary history worth pursuing: Ferrero

Guglielmo Ferrero’s *The Greatness and Decline of Rome* is a five-volume history whose relevant volumes are:

- III: *The Fall of an Aristocracy*
- IV: *Rome and Egypt*
- V: *The Republic of Augustus*. [archive](https://archive.org/details/cu31924020767939)

Volume IV is especially relevant to Antony and Cleopatra: its described coverage includes Antony’s Parthian strategy, his relationship with Cleopatra, and political tensions with Octavian. Volume V’s Google Books contents explicitly list an index beginning on page 401. [amazon](https://www.amazon.com/Greatness-Decline-Rome-4/dp/B009JTQ5B6)

Access points:

- [Internet Archive series record and digitized volume](https://archive.org/details/cu31924020767939).
- [Google Books: Volume V, *The Republic of Augustus*](https://books.google.com/books/about/The_Greatness_and_Decline_of_Rome_5_The.html?id=ubsoAAAAYAAJ).

A caution about access: the Archive record lists all five titles in its metadata, but that does not establish that all five are contained in the linked scan. Its download section also returned “Temporarily Unavailable” during this check. I would therefore treat Ferrero as a promising research lead rather than claim that this particular link gives you a verified, complete five-volume download. [archive](https://archive.org/details/cu31924020767939)

## Which to start with

For an indexed reference work, start with Shuckburgh’s *Augustus*. For a continuous narrative of Antony, Cleopatra, and Actium, start with chapters XV–XX of Weigall. Those two give you complementary approaches, and both offer accessible online text rather than merely catalog descriptions. [en.wikisource](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Augustus_(Shuckburgh))

For source-based research, the distinction matters: Shuckburgh provides a confirmed printed index, while Weigall provides unusually direct chapter access to the campaign but explicitly limits his scholarly apparatus. That makes the pair more useful together than either alone. [en.wikisource](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Augustus_(Shuckburgh))

## The closest match

### John Richard Hale — *Famous Sea Fights from Salamis to Tsu-Shima* (1911)

[Read the complete book free at Project Gutenberg](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/25088/25088-h/25088-h.htm).

- Actium chapter: printed pages 25–39.
- Focus: the political lead-up, opposing fleets, naval campaign, battle, and aftermath.
- Illustrations and plans: the book includes thirteen illustrations and seventeen plans.
- Navigation: the linked contents take you directly to “Actium, B.C. 31”; the HTML is searchable. I have not verified a separate subject index. [gutenberg](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/25088/25088-h/25088-h.htm)

This is the strongest verified match so far for your combination of public-domain, full-text online, secondary nonfiction, and specific attention to the battle. It is a chapter in a naval-history collection, not a standalone Actium monograph. Its age also matters: Hale frames Actium as a decisive battle that established Octavian’s imperial supremacy, so treat it as an older interpretation rather than a current scholarly reconstruction. [gutenberg](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/25088/25088-h/25088-h.htm)
