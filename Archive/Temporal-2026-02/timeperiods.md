The categorization of the past into discrete, quantified named blocks of time is called [periodization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Periodization "Periodization").[^2] This is a list of such **named time periods** as defined in various fields of study.

These can be divided broadly into prehistoric periods and historical periods (when written records began to be kept).

In [archaeology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Archaeology "Archaeology") and [anthropology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anthropology "Anthropology"), [prehistory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prehistory "Prehistory") is subdivided into the [three-age system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Three-age_system "Three-age system"). This list includes the use of the three-age system as well as a number of various designations used in reference to sub-ages within the traditional three.

The dates for each age can vary by region. On the [geologic time scale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geologic_time_scale "Geologic time scale"), the [Holocene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holocene "Holocene") epoch starts at the end of the [last glacial period of the current ice age](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Last_glacial_period "Last glacial period") (c. 10,000 BC) and continues to the present. The beginning of the Mesolithic is usually considered to correspond to the beginning of the [Holocene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holocene "Holocene") epoch.

- **[Prehistory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prehistory "Prehistory")** – Period between the appearance of *[Homo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homo "Homo")* ("humans"; [first stone tools](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oldowan "Oldowan") c. three million years ago) and the invention of writing systems (for the [Ancient Near East](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Near_East "Ancient Near East"): c. five thousand years ago).
	- [Paleolithic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paleolithic "Paleolithic") – the earliest period of the Stone Age
		- [Lower Paleolithic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lower_Paleolithic "Lower Paleolithic") – time of [archaic human](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Archaic_human "Archaic human") species, predates *[Homo sapiens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homo_sapiens "Homo sapiens")*
		- [Middle Paleolithic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Middle_Paleolithic "Middle Paleolithic") – coexistence of archaic and [anatomically modern human](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anatomically_modern_human "Anatomically modern human") species
		- [Upper Paleolithic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Upper_Paleolithic "Upper Paleolithic") – [worldwide expansion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Early_human_migrations "Early human migrations") of anatomically modern humans, the disappearance of archaic humans by extinction or [admixture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Archaic_human_admixture_with_modern_humans "Archaic human admixture with modern humans") with modern humans; earliest [evidence for pictorial art](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Art_of_the_Upper_Paleolithic "Art of the Upper Paleolithic").
	- [Mesolithic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mesolithic "Mesolithic") ([Epipaleolithic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Epipaleolithic "Epipaleolithic")) – a period in the development of human technology between the Palaeolithic and Neolithic periods.
	- [Neolithic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neolithic "Neolithic") – a period of primitive [technological](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Technological "Technological") and [social](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social "Social") development, beginning about 10,200 BC in parts of the Middle East, and later in other parts of the world.
	- [Chalcolithic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chalcolithic "Chalcolithic") (or "Eneolithic", "Copper Age") – still largely Neolithic in character, when early [copper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Copper "Copper") [metallurgy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metallurgy "Metallurgy") appeared alongside the use of stone tools.
	- [Bronze Age](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bronze_Age "Bronze Age") – not part of prehistory for all regions and civilizations who had adopted or developed a writing system.
	- [Iron Age](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iron_Age "Iron Age") – not part of prehistory for all civilizations who had introduced written records during the Bronze Age.
- **[Ancient history](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_history "Ancient history")** – Aggregate of past events from the beginning of recorded human history and extending as far as the Early Middle Ages or the Postclassical Era. The span of [recorded history](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Recorded_history "Recorded history") is roughly five thousand years, beginning with the earliest linguistic records in the third millennium BC in [Mesopotamia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sumer "Sumer") and [Egypt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Early_Dynastic_Period_\(Egypt\) "Early Dynastic Period (Egypt)").
	- **[Classical antiquity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Classical_antiquity "Classical antiquity")** – Broad term for a long period of cultural history centered on the Mediterranean Sea, comprising the interlocking civilizations of [ancient Greece](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Greece "Ancient Greece") and [ancient Rome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Rome "Ancient Rome"), collectively known as the [Greco-Roman world](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greco-Roman_world "Greco-Roman world"). It is the period in which Greek and Roman society flourished and wielded great influence throughout Europe, North Africa and the Middle East.
- **[Post-classical history](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Post-classical_history "Post-classical history")** – Period of time that immediately followed ancient history. Depending on the continent, the era generally falls between the years AD 200–600 and AD 1200–1500. The major classical civilizations that the era follows are [Han China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Han_dynasty "Han dynasty") (ending in 220), the [Western Roman Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Roman_Empire "Western Roman Empire") (in 476), the [Gupta Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gupta_Empire "Gupta Empire") (in the 550s), and the [Sasanian Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sasanian_Empire "Sasanian Empire") (in 651).
	- **[Middle Ages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Middle_Ages "Middle Ages")** – Lasted from the 5th to the 15th century. It began with the collapse of the Western Roman Empire in 476 and is variously demarcated by historians as ending with the [Fall of Constantinople](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fall_of_Constantinople "Fall of Constantinople") in 1453, or the discovery of America by Columbus in 1492, merging into the [Renaissance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renaissance "Renaissance") and the [Age of Discovery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Age_of_Discovery "Age of Discovery").
		- [Early Middle Ages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Early_Middle_Ages "Early Middle Ages")
		- [High Middle Ages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High_Middle_Ages "High Middle Ages")
		- [Late Middle Ages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Late_Middle_Ages "Late Middle Ages")
- **[Modern history](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Modern_history "Modern history")** – After the post-classical era
	- **[Early modern period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Early_modern_period "Early modern period")** – The chronological limits of this period are open to debate. It emerges from the [Late Middle Ages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Late_Middle_Ages "Late Middle Ages") (c. 1500), demarcated by historians as beginning with the [fall of Constantinople](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fall_of_Constantinople "Fall of Constantinople") in 1453, in forms such as the [Italian Renaissance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italian_Renaissance "Italian Renaissance") in the West, the [Ming dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ming_dynasty "Ming dynasty") in the East, and the rise of the [Aztecs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aztecs "Aztecs") in the New World. The period ends with the beginning of the [Age of Revolutions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Age_of_Revolutions "Age of Revolutions").
	- **[Contemporary history](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Contemporary_history "Contemporary history")** – History within living memory. It shifts forward with the generations, and today is the span of historic events from approximately 1945 that are immediately relevant to the present time.
